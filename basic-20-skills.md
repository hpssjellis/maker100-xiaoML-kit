Based on the Maker100-XIAOML curriculum and the specific hardware requirements of the Seeed Studio XIAO ML Kit, here is a list of 20 basic skills that high school students should ideally master (or be introduced to) before starting the course.

Since your curriculum emphasizes that "Robotics is fundamentally about solving technology problems," these skills focus on creating a foundation of technical literacy, spatial reasoning, and a "debugging" mindset.

## Digital & Software Literacy

1. File Management: Proficiency in creating folders, unzipping files, and understanding file paths (essential for the GitHub-heavy curriculum).

1. GitHub Basics: Ability to create an account, navigate a repository, and understand the difference between "cloning" and "downloading a zip."

1. Software Installation: Understanding how to install applications and handle administrative permissions (crucial for VS Code, Arduino IDE, and Python).

1. Typing Proficiency: Comfort with typing code accurately; frustration often stems from syntax errors caused by "hunt-and-peck" typing.

1. Search & Research Skills: Knowing how to use search engines or LLMs (like ChatGPT) to troubleshoot specific error messages.

1. Command Line Basics: Ability to open a terminal (PowerShell or Bash) and run simple commands like cd or dir/ls.

## Electronics Fundamentals

1. Concept of Polarity: Understanding that certain components (LEDs, batteries, capacitors) have a specific direction (Positive vs. Negative).

1. Breadboard Logic: Understanding how a breadboard’s internal rails are connected (vertical vs. horizontal rows).

1. Continuity & Circuits: Knowing what a "complete circuit" is and how a simple switch or wire creates or breaks that path.

1. Identifying Components: Recognizing basic parts like resistors, LEDs, and sensors by sight before they are integrated into a project.

1. Basic Units of Measure: Familiarity with Volts, Amps, and Ohms (and the metric prefixes: milli-, kilo-, micro-).

## Programming & Logic

1. Variable Logic: Understanding that a variable is a "bucket" that holds data (Integers vs. Strings vs. Booleans).

1. Conditional Thinking: Mastering "If-Then-Else" logic—essential for your Code03-if assignment.

1. Looping Concepts: Understanding repetitive actions (how a "For" or "While" loop works conceptually).

1. Coordinate Systems: Understanding X, Y, and Z axes (vital for both 3D printing and the 6-axis IMU in the XIAO kit).

## Hardware & Shop Safety

1. Soldering Safety & Basics: Understanding how to safely handle a 350°C tool and the importance of ventilation.

1. Tool Handling: Safe use of wire strippers, flush cutters, and perhaps a hot glue gun for building structures.

1. Component Precision: The ability to handle small, delicate parts (like the XIAO board) without damaging pins.

## Soft Skills & Documentation

1. Technical Documentation (Video/Notes): Since your course uses videos for marking, students should know how to record, edit, and narrate a clear "proof of work" video.

1. The Iterative Mindset: Accepting that failure is a feature. Students should be prepared for the fact that their code/circuit will likely not work the first time.

Why these specific 20?
Your curriculum is asynchronous and student-led. If a student starts without knowing how to manage files or what "polarity" is, they will get stuck on the very first installation and "Blink" assignments. Mastering these 20 skills allows them to focus on the high-level concepts of TinyML and IoT rather than getting tripped up by basic computer or electrical hurdles.
